# ADR-0005 — DNS: registrar at Fasthosts, nameservers at Cloudflare

- **Status:** Accepted — forced by the platform choice
- **Date:** 2026-08-06
- **Owner:** Platform volunteer
- **Related:** [ADR-0013](0013-delegation-approach.md) covers *how* the delegation is carried out — that decision is open

## Context

[ADR-0002](0002-hosting-platform.md) makes Cloudflare Workers the platform's host. The
club's first milestone is Nightingale Nightmare running at
`nn.southvillerunningclub.co.uk`, which means a Worker serving a club hostname.

**A Worker can only serve a hostname on a domain whose nameservers point at Cloudflare.**
This was checked properly, because it determines the whole running order:

- **Workers Custom Domains** require an active Cloudflare zone; you cannot create one on a
  zone you do not hold.
- **Workers Routes** likewise require a full zone and a proxied DNS record for the
  hostname.
- **Cloudflare's partial (CNAME) setup** — the mechanism for proxying individual hostnames
  while nameservers stay elsewhere — is **Business plan or above**, and even then it
  fronts an existing origin rather than letting Workers serve the hostname.
- **Cloudflare for SaaS** custom hostnames could be bent into shape, but it is a paid
  add-on used against its purpose — precisely the clever-over-boring that
  [P6](../principles.md#p6--boring-by-default) rules out.

**There is no paid escape hatch.** The Business plan costs around £1,900 a year — eight to
ten times the club's entire projected platform budget — and would not solve this. Spending
more money does not avoid delegation.

## Decision

**The domain stays registered at Fasthosts. The authoritative nameservers move to
Cloudflare.**

### Delegation is not migration

This distinction governs the whole programme, and conflating the two is the mistake to
avoid.

Moving nameservers changes **who answers DNS questions**, not **what the answers are**.
Copy every record across unchanged and Squarespace carries on serving the site, mail keeps
flowing, and no visitor can tell. Squarespace neither knows nor cares which nameserver gave
out the answer that pointed a browser at its IPs.

```
TODAY                                    AFTER DELEGATION
browser asks Fasthosts:                  browser asks Cloudflare:
  "where is www.southville...?"            "where is www.southville...?"
Fasthosts: "ext-cust.squarespace.com"    Cloudflare: "ext-cust.squarespace.com"
browser → Squarespace → site loads       browser → Squarespace → site loads
```

So the club's constraint — *nothing of Squarespace migrates until Nightingale Nightmare is
running on an SRC domain* — is satisfied by delegating first. Delegation is what **enables**
Nightingale Nightmare; the Squarespace migration sits untouched behind it.

### Two events, months apart

| | Event | Fails | Rollback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Delegation** — nameservers move, records copied 1:1 | **Invisibly** — mail stops, nobody notices for hours | **Slow** — up to 48 h |
| **2** | **Apex cutover** — apex records repointed to our Worker | **Visibly** — everyone sees it at once | **Fast** — seconds |

The risk lives in the first, quiet event. The second — the one that feels like the big
migration — is cheap and instantly reversible, because by then the club holds the records
and their TTLs.

### What delegation buys

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add a service hostname | Impossible on the club domain | One record, minutes |
| Move the apex | Nameserver-level, slow | One record, seconds |
| Roll that back | 24–48 h | Seconds |
| Stand up `beta.` for review | Impossible | One record |

Delegation is not an early migration. It is the step that makes every later move a toggle.

No CAA records. No AAAA records. No ALIAS or SRV records.

### The three things that will bite

**1. The MX points at a hostname inside the zone.** `mail.southvillerunningclub.co.uk` is
an A record here. Proxy it (orange cloud) and every inbound message resolves to Cloudflare,
which does not speak SMTP. Mail stops and nothing looks broken. Cloudflare defaults new A
records to proxied, so keeping this grey is an **active step**, not a default. This is the
highest-consequence record in the zone.

**2. Never click "restore automatic updates".** Two records carry that Fasthosts prompt.
Both were edited deliberately — the apex to point at Squarespace, the MX at
`mail.southvillerunningclub.co.uk`. "Restoring" the apex would repoint it at 88.208.252.9,
Fasthosts' own web hosting, and take the site down. **Replicate what is there, never what
Fasthosts suggests.**

**3. The Squarespace verification host is a random token**, `9sw9cgfs3d8e53r2xcx5`, not
`verify`. Easy to mistype, and it must stay DNS-only — proxy it and Squarespace cannot
verify domain ownership, which can disable custom-domain serving on the live site.

### Two things already in the club's favour

**No CAA records**, so no certificate authority restriction can block Cloudflare issuing
TLS certificates for the new hostnames. **DMARC at `p=none`** means no enforcement — if SPF
or DKIM are mangled during the move, mail degrades toward spam folders rather than being
rejected outright. Both remove whole categories of failure before anyone starts.

## Target hostnames

| Hostname | Serves | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `@` + `www` | Squarespace, unchanged | today |
| `nn.` | Nightingale Nightmare Worker | after delegation |
| `beta.` *(noindex)* | Rebuilt site Worker | during the rebuild |
| `timing.` | Timing Worker, parallel to Vercel | after the port |
| `@` + `www` | **Site Worker** | at the apex cutover |

**`www` stays canonical.** The bare domain 301-redirects to `www` today, and every printed
link, bookmark and search result points there. The cutover preserves that behaviour
exactly; changing it is a separate decision for a separate day.

**Timing takes a subdomain, not a path.** A path under the apex would put the website
Worker in charge of routing to race-day surfaces, so a website deploy could break timing.
That is precisely what
[P7](../principles.md#p7--race-day-is-safety-critical-the-website-is-not) forbids. A path
alias may be added after the apex is ours, redirecting to the subdomain, which keeps the
isolation while giving the nicer public URL.

## Consequences

- Cloudflare becomes authoritative for club **email routing** as well as the website. The
  blast radius of a DNS mistake is larger than the site, and the record-by-record audit is
  not optional.
- The Cloudflare account becomes critical club infrastructure. It must be **club-owned with
  more than one administrator** before it holds anything
  ([R1](../risks.md#r1--key-person-dependency)). A club-owned account is free; no paid plan
  is required for ownership.
- The **Fasthosts account is the rollback route**, so more than one person needs access to
  it before the change, not after.
- SPF's `a` mechanism authorises whatever the apex resolves to. Once the apex is a Worker
  that means Cloudflare's proxy IPs — harmless, since they send no mail, but untidy.
  **Review at the apex cutover, never during delegation.** One change at a time.
- DNS records are documented here. It remains a console-driven change and a knowing
  exception to [P1](../principles.md#p1--everything-is-code-nothing-is-clicked);
  Cloudflare's API or Terraform provider would close it once there is a second maintainer
  to benefit.
- Resend's sending-domain verification records slot into the same zone once delegated,
  closing the timing app's deferred custom-SMTP work.
- If Squarespace rather than Fasthosts holds the **registration**, a transfer carries a
  60-day lock and becomes the true first step. Confirm before scheduling anything.

## Revisit if

The club changes registrar; Cloudflare's DNS management proves limiting; or a second
maintainer makes infrastructure-as-code worth its overhead.
